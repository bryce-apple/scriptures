\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{TESTIMONY-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item A solemn declaration or affirmation made for the purpose of establishing or proving some fact. Such affirmation in judicial proceedings, may be verbal or written, but must be under oath. \textit{testimony} differs from evidence; \textit{testimony} is the declaration of a witness, and evidence is the effect of that declaration on the mind, or the degree of light which it affords.
		\item[1] Affirmation; declaration. These doctrines are supported by the uniform \textit{testimony} of the fathers. The belief of past facts must depend on the evidence of human \textit{testimony} or the \textit{testimony} of historians.
		\item[2] Open attestation; profession.
		\item[3] Witness; evidence; proof of some fact.
		\item[4] In Scripture, the two tables of the law.
		\item[5] The book of the law.
		\item[6] The gospel, which testifies of Christ and declares the will of God.
		\item[7] The ark.
		\item[8] The word of God; the Scriptures.
		\item[9] The laws or precepts of God.
		\item[10] That which is equivalent to a declaration; manifestation.
		\item[11] Evidence suggested to the mind; as the \textit{testimony} of conscience.
		\item[12] Attestation; confirmation.
	\end{compactitem}
