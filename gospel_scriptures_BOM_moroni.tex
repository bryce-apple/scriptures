			And the way to judge is as plain,
			that ye may know with a perfect knowledge,
			as the daylight is from the dark night.%
				\footnote{%
					\label{MORONI-7-15-order}%
					The order of the parts of this verse is a little confusing and obscures the comparison that Mormon is making. You could re-write the second half of this verse in this way to make it clearer: ``And the way to judge is as plain as the daylight is from the dark night, that ye may know with a perfect knowledge.'' The comparison is important---we're supposed to learn that the way to judge good from evil is as plain as the daylight is from the ``dark night.''
					
					The phrase ``dark night'' only occurs twice in the scriptures, and the other use is at \hyperref[PROVERBS-7-9]{Proverbs 7:9}, discussing a story of a kid visiting a harlot. I'm not sure how relevant that is for understanding this verse in Moroni, but it is interesting that you get ``dark night'' instead of just ``night,'' emphasizing the contrast in the judgment you make.

				\footnote{%
					\label{MORONI-9-6-NOTE-enemy}%
					See \hyperref[ENEMY-PHRASES]{here} for other uses of this interesting phrase ``enemy of all righteousness.'' Of the four total occurrences of this phrase in the BOM, only this one in Moroni 9:6 refers to ``\textit{the}'' enemy of all righteousness; the rest are ``an.'' I think this may be significant---there are many possible enemies of righteousness, but what is \textit{the} enemy of righteousness, the one that we must ultimately overcome, the only one with any true power over us in the end? It is not Satan, or the devil, or anything like that; it is ourselves. \textit{We ourselves} are the only real enemy of all righteousness in our own lives; only we stop ourselves from reaching toward and arriving at wherever we want to go in righteousness. Everyone and everything else only has power over us if we choose to give them that power, so the only enemy of all righteousness that concerns us in the end is ourselves.
					}
