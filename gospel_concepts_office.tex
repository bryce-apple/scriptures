\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{OFFICE-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] A particular duty, charge or trust conferred by public authority and for a public purpose; an employment undertaken by commission or authority from government or those who administer it.
		\item[2] A duty, charge or trust of a sacred nature, conferred by God himself; as the \textit{office} of priest, in the Old Testament; and that of the apostles, in the New Testament.
		\item[3] Duty or employment of a private nature; as the \textit{office} of a midwife.
		\item[4] That which is performed, intended or assigned to be done by a particular thing, or that which any thing is fitted to perform; answering to duty in intelligent beings.
		\item[5] Business; particular employment.
		\item[6] Act of good or ill voluntarily tendered; usually in a good sense; as kind offices; offices of pity; pious offices.
		\item[7] Act of worship.
		\item[8] Formulary of devotion.
		\item[9] A house or apartment in which public officers and others transact business; as the register's office; a lawyer's \textit{office}.
		\item[10] In architecture, an apartment appropriated for the necessary business or occasions of a palace or nobleman's house.
		\item[11] In the canon law, a benefice which has no jurisdiction annexed to it.
		\item[12] The person or persons entrusted with particular duties of a public nature.
	\end{compactitem}
