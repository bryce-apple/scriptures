\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{PLEAD-1828}
	
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Intransitive verb}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item In a general sense, to argue in support of a claim, or in defense against the claim of another.
			\item[1] In law, to present an answer to the declaration of a plaintiff; to deny the plaintiff's declaration and demand, or to allege facts which show that he ought not to recover in the suit. The plaintiff declares or alleges; the defendant \textit{pleads} to his declaration. The king or the state prosecutes an offender, and the offender \textit{pleads} not guilty, or confesses the charge.
			\item[2] To urge reasons for or against; to attempt to persuade one by argument or supplication; as, to \textit{plead} for the life of a criminal; to \textit{plead} in his favor; to \textit{plead} with a judge or with a father.
			\item[3] To supplicate with earnestness.
			\item[4] To urge; to press by operating on the passions.
		\end{compactitem}
		
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Transitive verb}
	% -----
	
		\begin{compactitem}
			\item To discuss, defend and attempt to maintain by arguments or reasons offered to the tribunal or person who has the power of determining; as, to \textit{plead} a cause before a court or jury. In this sense, argue is more generally used by lawyers.
			\item[1] To allege or adduce in proof, support or vindication. The law of nations may be \textit{pleaded} in favor of the rights of embassadors.
			\item[2] To offer in excuse.
			\item[3] To allege and offer in a legal plea or defense, or for repelling a demand in law; as, to \textit{plead} usury; to \textit{plead} a statute of limitations.
			\item[4] In Scripture, to \textit{plead} the cause of the righteous, as God, is to avenge or vindicate them against enemies, or to redress their grievances.
		\end{compactitem}
