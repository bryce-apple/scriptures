\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{DEGREE-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] A step; a distinct portion of space of indefinite extent; a space in progression; as, the army gained the hill by \textit{degrees}; a balloon rises or descends by slow \textit{degrees}; and figuratively, we advance in knowledge by slow \textit{degrees}. Men are yet in the first \textit{degree} of improvement. It should be their aim to attain to the furthest \textit{degree} or the highest \textit{degree} There are \textit{degrees} of vice and virtue.
		\item[2] A step or portion of progression, in elevation, quality, dignity or rank; as a man of great \textit{degree}. We speak of men of high \textit{degree} or of low \textit{degree}; of superior or inferior \textit{degree} It is supposed there are different \textit{degrees} or orders of angels.
		\item[3] In genealogy, a certain distance or remove in the line of descent, determining the proximity of blood; as a relation in the third or fourth \textit{degree}.
		\item[4] Measure; extent. The light is intense to a \textit{degree} that is intolerable. We suffer an extreme \textit{degree} of heat or cold.
		\item[5] In geometry, a division of a circle, including a three hundred and sixtieth part of its circumference. Hence a \textit{degree} of latitude is the 360th part of the earths surface north or south of the equator, and a \textit{degree} of longitude, the same part of the surface east or west of any given meridian.
		\item[6] In music, an interval of sound, marked by a line on the scale.
		\item[7] In arithmetic, a \textit{degree} consists of three figures; thus, 270, 360, compose two \textit{degrees}.
		\item[8] A division, space or interval, marked on a mathematical or other instrument; as on a thermometer, or barometer.
		\item[9] In colleges and universities, a mark of distinction conferred on students, as a testimony of their proficiency in arts and sciences; giving them a kind of rank, and entitling them to certain privileges. This is usually evidenced by a diploma. \textit{degrees} are conferred pro meritis on the alumni of a college; or they are honorary tokens of respect, conferred on strangers of distinguished reputation. The first \textit{degree} is that of Bachelor of Arts; the second, that of Master of Arts. Honorary \textit{degrees} are those of Doctor of Divinity, Doctor of Laws, etc. Physicians also receive the \textit{degree} of Doctor of Medicine.
		\item By degrees, step by step; gradually; by little and little; by moderate advances. Frequent drinking forms by degrees a confirmed habit of intemperance.
	\end{compactitem}
