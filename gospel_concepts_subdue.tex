\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{SUBDUE-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] To conquer by force or the exertion of superior power, and bring into permanent subjection; to reduce under dominion. Thus Cesar subdued the Gauls; Augustus subdued Egypt; the English subdued Canada. Subduing implies conquest or vanquishing, but it implies also more permanence of subjection to the conquering power, than either of these words.
		\item[2] To oppress; to crush; to sink; to overpower so as to disable from further resistance.
		\item[3] To tame; to break by conquering a refractory temper or evil passions; to render submissive; as, to \textit{subdue} a stubborn child.
		\item[4] To conquer; to reduce to mildness; as, to \textit{subdue} the temper or passions.
		\item[5] To overcome by persuasion or other mild means; as, to \textit{subdue} opposition by argument or intreaties.
		\item[6] To overcome; to conquer; to captivate; as by charms.
		\item[7] To soften; to melt; to reduce to tenderness; as, to \textit{subdue} ferocity by tears.
		\item[8] To overcome; to overpower and destroy the force of; as, medicines \textit{subdue} a fever.
		\item[9] To make mellow; to break; as land; also, to destroy, as weeds.
	\end{compactitem}
