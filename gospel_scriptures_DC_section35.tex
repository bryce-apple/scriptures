	% -----
	\subsubsection{Symonds Rider version}
	% -----
	
		The JSP version of this section comes from RB1. But there is another early version, as noted by the JSP: ``A portion of another early copy of this revelation in the handwriting of David Whitmer survived through the Symonds Rider family, coming into the Church Historian's Office in the 1960s. Symonds Rider supplied the text to the editor of the \textit{Ohio Star} (Ravenna), which published it in the 5 January 1832 issue. Rider claimed that he received his version from David Whitmer. The text featured here, the Star version, and the existing portion of the Whitmer version from the Rider family are all similar, with no significant variants.''
		
		The ``Rider'' alternate version below comes from this source; see the entire text of that version \hyperref[EXSS-DC35-RIDER]{here}.
	
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Historical Background}
	% -----
		\label{DC35-HB}
		
		% --
		\paragraph{JSP}
		% --
		
			``The conversion of Sidney Rigdon and others in northeastern Ohio in fall 1830 transformed the new Church of Christ geographically and demographically. Within a few weeks after missionaries arrived in Ohio, more than one hundred individuals were baptized, and by the end of December church members in New York were commanded to move to Ohio. Kirtland, where the first baptisms in Ohio took place, became an important gathering place for church members for the next seven years. The visit of Sidney Rigdon and Edward Partridge to New York only a few weeks after Rigdon's baptism helped bring about this transition. According to John Whitmer's history, Rigdon made the trip because he had `much anxiety to see Joseph Smith Jr. the Seer whom the Lord had raised up in these last days.' Rigdon and Partridge arrived in early December 1830, and shortly thereafter JS dictated a revelation for each of them. This revelation, for Rigdon, praised his previous ministerial labors and positioned him as JS's scribe for JS's inspired translation of the Bible. Rigdon remained in New York with JS for the next two months until they both moved to Ohio.''

	\subsubsection{Section 35:2} % *DC35-2 
	% -----
		\label{DC35-2}

		I am Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who was crucified for the sins of the world, even as many as will believe on my name, that they may become the sons of God, even one%
			\footnote{%
				% \label{}%
				What does ``one'' mean here? I want to say it means ``one son,'' but I don't know how well that makes sense of Christ later saying (here) that ``the Father is one in me.'' Compare \hyperref[JOHN-10-30]{John 10:30}, \hyperref[JOHN-17-11]{John 17:11},\hyperref[JOHN-17-21]{John 17:21}, and possibly \hyperref[1JOHN-5-7]{1 John 5:7}. See also \hyperref[DC93-17]{DC 93:17} and verses around there.
				}
		in me%
			\footnote{%
				% \label{}%
				The phrase ``one in me'' occurs three times in the scriptures---twice in this verse, and once at \hyperref[MOSES-6-68]{Moses 6:68}, which is about the same idea. Note that verses 67--68 in Moses 6 talk about this ``becoming'' and make it clearer that it involves a specific ``order,'' or system of organization in the priesthod.
				}
		as I am one in the Father, as the Father is one in me, that we may be one.

		\begin{framed}
		\scriptsize
			\begin{compactdesc}
				\item[JSP] I am Jesus Christ the son of God who was crusified for the sins of the World even as will believe on my name that they may become the sons of God even one in me as I am in the Father as the Father is one in me that we may be one
				\item[Rider] I am Jesus Christ the Son of God, who was crucified for the sins of the world, even as many as will believe on my name, that they may become the sons of God, even one in me as I am in the Father, as the Father is one in me, that we may be one.
				\item[BC] 2 I am Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who was crucified for the sins of the world, even as many [a]s will believe on my name, that they may become the sons of God, even one in me as I am in the Father, as the Father is one in me, that we may be one.
				% \item[]
			\end{compactdesc}
		\end{framed}
