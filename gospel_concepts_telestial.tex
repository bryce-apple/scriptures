\subsection{Oxford English Dictionary} % *OED
% ----------------------
	\label{TELESTIAL-OED}

	``Telestial'' does not appear in this dictionary (!).
	
	There is an adjective, ``telestic,'' which the OED lists as meaning ``Of or relating to religious mysteries; mystical.'' The first use listed is from 1662 and there are uses in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries as well. This English word is a borrowing from Greek sources: \gr{τελεστικός} in Aristotle is ``fit for finishing or accomplishing,'' but also ``connected with mystic rites'' in Plato and other authors; a \gr{τελεστικόν} is ``payment for admission to a priesthood,'' \gr{τελεστής} is an ``initiator, priest,'' and \gr{τελετή} is a ``rite,'' especially ``initiation in the mysteries,'' or ``a festival accompanied by mystic rites,'' or ``a priesthood or sacred office'' (all definitions from LSJ).
