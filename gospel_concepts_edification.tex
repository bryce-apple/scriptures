\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{EDIFICATION-1828}
	
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Edification}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1] A building up, in a moral and religious sense; instruction; improvement and progress of the mind, in knowledge, in morals, or in faith and holiness.
			\item[2] Instruction; improvement of the mind in any species of useful knowledge.
		\end{compactitem}
		
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Edify}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1] To build, in a literal sense. [Not now used.]
			\item[2] To instruct and improve the mind in knowledge generally, and particularly in moral and religious knowledge, in faith and holiness.
			\item[3] To teach or persuade. [Not used.]
		\end{compactitem}

\subsection{Oxford English Dictionary} % *OED
% ----------------------
	\label{EDIFICATION-OED}
	
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Edify}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1.a] \textit{transitive.} To build; to construct (a dwelling, edifice) of the usual building materials. rare in modern use.
			\item[1.b] \textit{absolute.}
				\begin{compactitem}
					\item An absolute use of meaning \#1.a, that is.
				\end{compactitem}
			\item[1.c] To furnish with buildings.
			\item[2.a] To construct, set up, irrespective of the object, or kind of materials. \textit{Obsolete} or \textit{archaic.}
			\item[2.b] To work up or fashion (materials) into a building or structure.
			\item[2.c] \textit{figurative.} To build up, establish, organize (a system, institution, or law, a moral quality, etc.), to establish or strengthen (a person).
			\item[2.d] To frame a notion; `make out', imagine.
				\begin{compactitem}
					\item Only a single use given, from Milton in 1645.
				\end{compactitem}
			\item[2.e] \textit{intransitive.} To take form, grow; also figurative to prosper, achieve success. Cf. 4. \textit{Obsolete.}
			\item[3.a] \textit{transitive.} In religious use: To build up (the church, the soul) in faith and holiness; to benefit spiritually; to strengthen, support. Also \textit{absol.}
			\item[3.b] To inform, instruct; to improve in a moral sense; sometimes \textit{ironical.}
			\item[4.a] \textit{intransitive.} To profit in a spiritual sense. \textit{Obsolete.}
			\item[4.b] To gain instruction generally. \textit{Obsolete.}
		\end{compactitem}

\subsection{Buck's Theological Dictionary (1833)} % *BTD
% ----------------------
	\label{EDIFICATION-BTD}
	
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Edification}
	% -----

		\begin{compactdesc}
			\item[319] This word signifies a building up. Hence we call a building an edifice. Applied to spiritual things, it signifies the improving, adorning, and comforting the mind; and a Christian may be said to be edified when he is encouraged and animated in the ways and works of the Lord. The means to promote our own edification are, prayer, self-examination, reading the Scriptures, hearing the Gospel, meditation, attendance on all appointed ordinances. To edify others, there should be love, spiritual conversation, forbearance, faithfulness, benevolent exertions, and uniformity of conduct.
		\end{compactdesc}
