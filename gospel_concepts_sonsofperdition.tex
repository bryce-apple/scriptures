	% -----
	\subsubsection{Perdition}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1] Entire loss or ruin; utter destruction; as the \textit{perdition} of the Turkish fleet. [In this sense, the word is now nearly or wholly obsolete.]
			\item[2] The utter loss of the soul or of final happiness in a future state; future misery or eternal death. The impenitent sinner is condemned to final \textit{perdition} If we reject the truth, we seal our own \textit{perdition}.
			\item[3] Loss. [Not used.]
		\end{compactitem}

				\item[{\hyperref[JOHN-18-9]{John 18:9}}] \phantom{.}
					\begin{compactitem}
						\item In this verse Christ uses \gr{ἀπώλεσα} to talk about those that he is supposed to keep. That's the verb related to \gr{ἀπώλεια}, the noun used to talk about perdition (see below). So I'm wondering if part of what makes the sons of perdition who they are is that they refuse Christ's salvation and therefore elect on their own account to not be part of the group that Christ doesn't lose. He saves everything else, but not them, because they refuse it. Note that the ``heavens weep'' in DC 76 over them.
					\end{compactitem}
				\item[{\hyperref[JOHN-19-14]{John 19:14--15}}] \phantom{.}
					\begin{compactitem}
						\item These have nothing to do with the sons of perdition exactly, but the attitude of the priests here just has a certain feeling about it to me that reminds me of things said about the sons of perdition.
