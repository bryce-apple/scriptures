	\subsubsection{Earnest (noun)}
	% -----
	
		\begin{compactitem}
			\item Seriousness; a reality; a real event; as opposed to jesting or feigned appearance.
			\item[1] First fruits; that which is in advance, and gives promise of something to come. Early fruit may be an \textit{earnest} of fruit to follow. The first success in arms may be an \textit{earnest} of future success. The christian's peace of mind in this life is an \textit{earnest} of future peace and happiness. Hence \textit{earnest} or \textit{earnest}-money is a first payment or deposit giving promise or assurance of full payment. Hence the practice of giving an \textit{earnest} to ratify a bargain.

			This sense of the word is primary, denoting that which goes before, or in advance. Thus the \textit{earnest} of the spirit is given to saints, as a pledge or assurance of their future enjoyment of God's presence and favor.
		\end{compactitem}

					\item[\textdagger\ TDNT 1:475] \phantom{.}
						\begin{compactitem}
							\item A loan word from the Semitic, Heb. \he{`erAbON}. \hyperref[GENESIS-38-17]{Gn. 38:17 ff.} (LXX, \gr{ἀρραβών}), Lat. \textit{arrha} or \textit{arrhabo}...the word is a commercial term (Isaeus 8:23); Aristot. Pol., I, 11, p. 1259a, 12; esp. pap.). It signifies a ``pledge'' which is later returned (only Gn. 38:17--20); a ``deposit'' which pays part of the total debt and gives a legal claim...``earnest-money'' ratifying a compact...it always implies an act which engages to something bigger...
							\item Paul uses it figuratively at 2 C. 1:22: \gr{ὁ...δοὺς τὸν ἀρραβῶνα τοῦ πνεύματος ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ἡμῶν}; 5:5: \gr{ὁ δοὺς ἡμῖν τὸν ἀρραβῶνα τοῦ πνεύματος}. In the latter \gr{τοῦ πνεύματος} is genitive appositive: ``the earnest, i.e., the Spirit'' (like \gr{ἀπαρχὴ τοῦ πνεύματος} in \hyperref[ROMANS-8-23]{R. 8:23}). The Spirit whom God has given them is for Christians the guarantee of their full future possession of salvation. Similarly in Eph. 1:14: \gr{τῷ πνεύματι...ὅς ἐστιν ἀρραβὼν τῆς κληρονομίας ἡμῶν}.
							\item In 2nd century Christian literature it is found only in Pol., 8, 1: \gr{τῷ ἀρραβῶνι τῆς δικαιοσύνης ἡµῶν ὅς ἐστι Χριστὸς Ἰησοῦς}, Christ by His death is a pledge that  Christians will attain to righteousness at the last judgment.
						\end{compactitem}
