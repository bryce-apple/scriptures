\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{PRINCIPLE-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] In a general sense, the cause, source or origin of any thing; that from which a thing proceeds; as the \textit{principle} of motion; the \textit{principles} of action.
		\item[2] Element; constituent part; primordial substance. Modern philosophers suppose matter to be one simple \textit{principle} or solid extension diversified by its various shapes.
		\item[3] Being that produces any thing; operative cause. The soul of man is an active \textit{principle}
		\item[4] In science, a truth admitted either without proof, or considered as having been before proved. In the former sense, it is synonymous with axiom; in the latter, with the phrase, established \textit{principle}.
		\item[5] Ground; foundation; that which supports an assertion, an action, or a series of actions or of reasoning. On what \textit{principle} can this be affirmed or denied? He justifies his proceedings on the \textit{principle} of expedience or necessity. He reasons on sound \textit{principles}.
		\item[6] A general truth; a law comprehending many subordinate truths; as the \textit{principles} of morality, of law, of government, etc.
		\item[7] Tenet; that which is believed, whether truth or not, but which serves as a rule of action or the basis of a system; as the \textit{principles} of the Stoics, or of the Epicureans.
		\item[8] A \textit{principle} of human nature, is a law of action in human beings; a constitutional propensity common to the human species. Thus it is a \textit{principle} of human nature to resent injuries and repel insults.
	\end{compactitem}
