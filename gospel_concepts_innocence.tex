	% -----
	\subsubsection{Innocence}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1] Properly, freedom from any quality that can injure; innoxiousness; harmlessness; as the \textit{innocence} of a medicine which can do no harm. In this sense, the noun is not obsolete, though less used than the adjective.
			\item[2] In a moral sense, freedom from crime, sin or guilt; untainted purity of heart and life; unimpaired integrity.
			\item[3] Freedom from guilt or evil intentions; simplicity of heart; as the \textit{innocence} of a child.
			\item[4] Freedom from the guilt of a particular sin or crime. This is the sense in which the word is most generally used, for perfect \textit{innocence} cannot be predicated of man. A man charged with theft or murder may prove his \textit{innocence}
			\item[5] Freedom from the guilt of a particular sin or crime. This is the sense in which the word is most generally used, for perfect \textit{innocence} cannot be predicated of man. A man charged with theft or murder may prove his \textit{innocence}
		\end{compactitem}
		
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Innocent}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1] Properly, not noxious; not producing injury; free from qualities that can injure; harmless; innoxious; as an \textit{innocent} medicine or remedy.
			\item[2] Free from guilt; not having done wrong or violated any law; not tainted with sin; pure; upright. In this general sense, no human being that is a moral agent, can be \textit{innocent}. It is followed by of.
			\item[3] Free from the guilt of a particular crime or evil action; as, a man is \textit{innocent} of the crime charged in the indictment.
			\item[4] Lawful; permitted; as an \textit{innocent} trade.
			\item[5] Not contraband; not subject to forfeiture; as \textit{innocent} goods carried to a belligerent nation.
		\end{compactitem}
