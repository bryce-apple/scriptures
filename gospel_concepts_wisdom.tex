\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{WISDOM-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] The right use or exercise of knowledge; the choice of laudable ends, and of the best means to accomplish them. This is \textit{wisdom} in act, effect, or practice. If \textit{wisdom} is to be considered as a faculty of the mind, it is the faculty of discerning or judging what is most just, proper and useful, and if it is to be considered as an acquirement, it is the knowledge and use of what is best, most just, most proper, most conducive to prosperity or happiness. \textit{Wisdom} in the first sense, or practical \textit{wisdom} is nearly synonymous with discretion. It differs somewhat from prudence, in this respect; prudence is the exercise of sound judgment in avoiding evils; \textit{wisdom} is the exercise of sound judgment either in avoiding evils or attempting good. Prudence then is a species, of which \textit{wisdom} is the genus.
		\item[2] In Scripture, human learning; erudition; knowledge of arts and sciences.
		\item[3] Quickness of intellect; readiness of apprehension; dexterity in execution; as the \textit{wisdom} of Bezaleel and Aholiab. 
		\item[4] Natural instinct and sagacity. 
		\item[5] In Scripture theology, \textit{wisdom} is true religion; godliness; piety; the knowledge and fear of God, and sincere and uniform obedience to his commands.
		\item[6] Profitable words or doctrine.
	\end{compactitem}
