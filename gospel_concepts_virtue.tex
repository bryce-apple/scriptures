\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{VIRTUE-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] Strength; that substance or quality of physical bodies, by which they act and produce effects on other bodies. In this literal and proper sense, we speak of the \textit{virtue} or \textit{virtue}s of plants in medicine, and the \textit{virtue}s of drugs. In decoctions, the \textit{virtue}s of plants are extracted. By long standing in the open air, the \textit{virtue}s are lost.
		\item[2] Bravery valor. This was the predominant signification of virtus among the Romans.
		\item[3] Moral goodness; the practice of moral duties and the abstaining from vice, or a conformity of life and conversation to the moral law. In this sense, \textit{virtue} may be, and in many instances must be, distinguished from religion. The practice of moral duties merely from motives of convenience, or from compulsion, or from regard to reputation, is \textit{virtue} as distinct from religion. The practice of moral duties from sincere love to God and his laws, is \textit{virtue} and religion. In this sense it is true, That \textit{virtue} only makes our bliss below. \textit{Virtue} is nothing but voluntary obedience to truth.
		\item[4] A particular moral excellence; as the \textit{virtue} of temperance, of chastity, of charity. Remember all his \textit{virtue}s.
		\item[5] Acting power; something efficacious. Jesus, knowing that \textit{virtue} had gone out of him, turned - Mark 3:1.
		\item[6] Secret agency; efficacy without visible or material action. She moves the body which she doth possess, Yet no part toucheth, but by \textit{virtue}'s touch.
		\item[7] Excellence; or that which constitutes value and merit. - Terence, who thought the sole grace and \textit{virtue} of their fable, the sticking in of sentences.
		\item[8] One of the orders of the celestial hierarchy. Thrones, dominations, princedoms, \textit{virtue}s, powers.
		\item[9] Efficacy; power. He used to travel through Greece by \textit{virtue} of this fable, which procured him reception in all the towns.
		\item[10] Legal efficacy or power; authority. A man administers the laws by \textit{virtue} of a commission. In \textit{virtue} in consequence; by the efficacy or authority. This they shall attain, partly in \textit{virtue} of the promise of God, and partly in \textit{virtue} of piety.
	\end{compactitem}
