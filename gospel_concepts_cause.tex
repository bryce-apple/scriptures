\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{CAUSE-1828}
	
	% -----
	\subsubsection{Noun}
	% -----

		\begin{compactitem}
			\item[1] A suit or action in court; any legal process which a party institutes to obtain his demand, or by which he seeks his right or his supposed right. This is a legal, scriptural and popular use of the word, coinciding nearly with ``case'' from \textit{cado}, and ``action'' from \textit{ago}, to urge or drive.
			\item[2] That which produces an effect; that which impels into existence, or by its agency or operation produces what did not before exist; that by virtue of which any thing is done; that from which any thing proceeds, and without which it would not exist.
			\item[3] The reason or motive that urges, moves, or impels the mind to act or decide.
			\item[4] Sake; account.
			\item[5] That which a party or nation pursues; or rather pursuit, prosecution of an object. We say, Bible Societies are engaged in a noble \textit{cause} [See the first definition.] Hence the word \textit{cause} is used to denote that which a person or thing favors; that to which the efforts of an intelligent being are directed; as, to promote religion is to advance the \textit{cause} of God. So we say, the \textit{cause} of truth or of justice. In all its applications, \textit{cause} retains something of its original meaning, struggle, impelling force, contest, effort to obtain or to effect something.
			\item[6] Without \textit{cause} without good reason; without a reason or motive to justify the act.
		\end{compactitem}
