\subsection{1828 American Dictionary of the English Language} % *1828
% ----------------------
	\label{SPHERE-1828}

	\begin{compactitem}
		\item[1] In geometry, a solid body contained under a single surface, which in every part is equally distant from a point called its center.
		\item[2] An orb or globe of the mundane system. First the sun, a mighty \textit{sphere} he fram'd. Then mortal ears had heard the music of the \textit{spheres}.
		\item[3] An orbicular body, or a circular figure representing the earth or apparent heavens.
		\item[4] Circuit of motion; revolution; orbit; as the diurnal \textit{sphere}.
		\item[5] The concave or vast orbicular expanse in which the heavenly orbs appear.
		\item[6] Circuit of action, knowledge or influence; compass; province; employment. Every man has his particular \textit{sphere} of action, in which it should be his ambition to excel. Events of this kind have repeatedly fallen within the \textit{sphere} of my knowledge. This man treats of matters not within his \textit{sphere}.
		\item[7] Rank; order of society. Persons moving in a higher \textit{sphere} claim more deference..
	\end{compactitem}
